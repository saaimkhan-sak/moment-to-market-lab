UNDERSTANDING PUBLIC MOMENTUM AFTER KEY NHL MOMENTS

What NHL moments drive the most public engagement?

Every hockey fan understands the indescribable feeling of following the buzz after incredible, season-defining moments: whether it's an overtime winner, a hat trick, a four-point night, or an incredible 3rd-period comeback. But, behind all of that, I wanted to understand:
What exactly is the pattern of that momentum across the league and how much of it is harnessable?

01THE GENESIS

Initially, I wanted to look at club revenues after key in-season moments.
But, there's not enough available public data to answer that question

As I became more fascinated with the business behind the NHL, I was intrigued by the question of: could the data tell us which hockey moments sell the most tickets, move the most merchandise, create the most sponsor value, or even change fan behavior?

But, the useful data to that question is unfortunately not publicly available - it's housed in proprietary ticketing systems, commerce platforms, partnership agreements, and club-specific databases. So instead, I pivoted and found the next best proxy of publicly available data - the public attention that follows those moments.

So I changed the question instead of stretching the evidence. Public data can show when people seek information and when a club enters the news conversation. It can show whether that response arrives immediately, lingers for a few days, or disappears. That is narrower than commercial value—and far more defensible.

THE PIVOT

Shifting from private to public data.

  • Identify notable moments from the NHL news public record.
  • Use sentiment analysis of social media signals to follow the subsequent public attention.
  • Compare the first 48 hours, the next two days, and the rest of the week.
02THE WALKTHROUGH

Step-By-Step Method

01

BUILD THE GAME RECORD

The game record is established before any attention response is considered.

NHL schedules, box scores, play-by-play, rosters, and standings are used to establish the game, clock, score, players, and opponent. MoneyPuck is used to add expected-goal and game-state context. The sources are reconciled at the game level rather than treated as interchangeable.

INPUTgame_id + event_id + event_time_utc

Each raw response is retained with its source URL, retrieval time, and checksum.

02

DEFINE THE MOMENT FIRST

The moment is defined before its public response is examined.

An overtime win is defined by the final game state; a hat trick by three goals; and a comeback by a club trailing by at least two goals at the start of the third period and winning the game. The rules are applied across the full archive, so memorable examples are not selected after attention data has been observed.

EXAMPLE LOGICif goals(player, game) ≥ 3
then moment = hat_trick

A new moment type is admitted only after a written rule, version increment, complete rebuild, and test case have been supplied.

03

RESOLVE ENTITY AND TIME

Club identity and temporal validity are resolved before records are joined.

Names, relocations, and dates are kept as separate dimensions. Wikidata is used to connect each club and player to the appropriate Wikipedia entity, including redirects and aliases. Arizona and Utah are retained as separate historical identities. Vegas, Seattle, and Utah are entered only for periods in which each franchise existed.

IDENTITY KEYclub_id + franchise_id
+ valid_from + valid_through

All event times are stored in UTC; local time is derived separately.

04

ESTABLISH A FAIR BASELINE

Each moment is compared with the club’s own preceding two weeks.

For every qualifying moment, the prior 14 days are used to establish the club’s normal level of public attention. This prevents a continuously active club from appearing important solely because it is continuously active. Missing days are retained as missing values rather than recoded as zero.

PUBLIC-ATTENTION DIFFERENCE
response during the window − average of the prior 14 days
max(average of the prior 14 days, 1)

A value of +0.20 is interpreted as a public signal 20% above its recent baseline. It is not interpreted as 20% more people or 20% more sales.

05

SEPARATE THE RESPONSE WINDOWS

The immediate burst and subsequent persistence are treated as separate windows.

Immediate response is assessed over the first two days. Short persistence is assessed on days two and three. Sustained attention is assessed over days four through seven.

2–3NEXT TWO DAYS
4–7REST OF WEEK
06

RETAIN THE SIGNALS SEPARATELY

Information seeking and earned-news presence are treated as distinct measures.

Information seeking is represented by daily Wikipedia page visits. Earned-news presence is represented by GDELT coverage after exact-name matching and a manual precision audit. The measures are analyzed separately because they describe different forms of public behavior.

WIKIPEDIAINFORMATION SEEKING
GDELTEARNED-NEWS PRESENCE

Neither measure should be interpreted as sentiment, readership, or purchase intent.

07

ADJUST FOR GAME CONTEXT

The conditions surrounding each NHL game are accounted for before club comparisons are made.

Because games occur under different conditions, the league-wide partial-pooling model is adjusted for home or road, opponent, standings context, day of week, month, season, and each club’s usual level. Information is shared across clubs when samples are thin, while club-level differences are retained.

REGISTERED FORMlog(attention + 1) ~ moment × window
+ home/away + opponent + standings context
+ day of week + month + season
+ club baseline + opponent baseline

Wikipedia and GDELT are fitted separately. Raw club medians are reported beside the adjusted estimates.

08

HANDLE OVERLAPPING MOMENTS

Overlapping moments are handled as potential confounds.

Where another major moment occurs within seven days, the event is excluded from the isolated before-and-after comparison. It is retained in the daily analysis, where both moment windows can be modeled together. The reason for exclusion is kept visible.

OVERLAP RULEif |day(A) − day(B)| ≤ 7
then isolated(A) = false

The system never pretends one event acted alone when another major story occupied the same week.

09

DEFINE WHEN A PATTERN IS SUPPORTED

The two signals must agree before a repeatable label is assigned.

At least 10 comparable events are required for each source, and all evidence thresholds must be cleared. The adjusted result and the club’s raw history must indicate the same direction, while their uncertainty ranges must remain entirely on one side of no change. Where any condition fails, the result is reported as mixed or not yet clear.

A REPEATABLE PUBLIC PATTERN REQUIRESNadjusted ≥ 10 ∧ Nclean ≥ 10
directionWikipedia = directionnews

Alternative 7-day and 21-day baselines are also checked before a finding is released.

03FROM EVIDENCE TO ACTION

The result is not treated as a prediction. It is used to frame a better-timed question.

If a recurring public aftershock is observed after a moment, a content test can be prepared around the relevant window. The public record can indicate when attention should be examined; whether the response worked can be established only with the club’s own data.

FIRST 24 HOURS

Publish while the story is alive.

Lead with the decisive sequence and the clearest explanation of why it mattered.

24–72 HOURS

Give the moment a second chapter.

Add context, personality, or a breakdown rather than repeating the highlight.

DAY 4–7

Check whether anything remained.

Compare the public trail and the club’s private measures before making the format routine.

THE HONEST PROMISE

This project does not tell a club what a moment was worth. It tells them when the public was still paying attention—and shows every step behind that answer.
